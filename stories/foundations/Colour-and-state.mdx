# Colour and state

The interface is monochrome. There is **one** colour in it, `destructive`, for what stops something and is for you to fix. Everything else is told in form: a symbol and words. Six `series-*` hues exist for a chart that is given colours of its own; they carry no meaning and are never a state. The values, their pairs and their measured contrast are in **Tokens / Overview**.

## 1. Roles

| Token | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `background`, `foreground` | The page and its text |
| `muted`, `muted-foreground` | Quiet surfaces and secondary text |
| `primary`, `primary-foreground` | The one strong action |
| `secondary`, `accent` | Hover and selected surfaces |
| `destructive` | What stops something |
| `border`, `input`, `ring` | Lines, field edges, the focus ring |
| `chart-1` to `chart-5` | A grey ramp for chart marks (see Data visualisation and Tokens / Overview) |
| `series-1` to `series-6` | Six hues for a chart given colours of its own; never a state |
| `temperature-warm`, `temperature-cold`, `precipitation` | The weather's marks in a `Meteogram`: the temperature above and below 0°, and what falls; never a state |
| `event-accent`, `event-success`, `event-warning`, `event-danger` | The node of an event in a `Timeline`, a step in `StateSteps` and a bar in `DurationBars` (merged or marked, went well, warns, failed), always with its icon and words |
| `transit-*` (bus, tram, metro, rail, ferry, other), `transit-late` | A public transport line's kind (`LineBadge`, always with its symbol), and a late departure's delay; never a state of anything else |

A page uses the tokens and never a colour of its own. Light and dark are the same tokens with other values.

## 2. State is never colour alone

A state has **a symbol, a word and a place**: <Badge icon="failure" tone="destructive">Feilet</Badge> <Badge icon="pull-request-draft">Utkast</Badge>
<Badge tone="muted">Arkivert</Badge>. Use one word for one meaning across the page, and put the symbol before the word. Attention goes from
what needs the reader now (`destructive`), through feedback and progress, to what only changed.

## 3. Contrast

Text is 4.5:1 against its background, and 3:1 for large text and for the parts of controls and marks that carry meaning (Atlassian, WCAG). The
table in **Tokens / Overview** has the measured ratio of every pair, in light and dark. Secondary text (`muted-foreground`) is 6.0:1 on the page and 5.5:1 on a muted
surface in light. `border` is 1.3:1 in light, so a line between parts is never the only edge of something the reader must find.

## 4. Selected and focused

A selected item and a focused item are different things. When both apply, both show (Atlassian): the focus ring is never removed from the selected one.

## 5. Dark mode

Dark is **not the light theme inverted**: a raised surface is lighter than the page (`card` over `background`), colours have less chroma, and neither the page nor the text is pure black or white. Check a pair on `card` as well as the page. An alpha colour
(`border`, `input`, the default chart steps) resolves against whatever is behind it, so its contrast changes from surface to surface: alpha never carries text or a mark that needs 3:1.

## 6. When the reader asks for something else

- **Forced colours** (Windows high contrast): the browser replaces colours and drops shadows and background images, so a state told only by a fill or a shadow is lost. The system makes small repairs in `shell.css` (a real focus outline, an edge on a control that is only a fill, a line for the current place, a visible fill for a meter). Icons use `currentColor` and follow the text. A new component with a shadow-only edge or a fill-only state also gets a border.
- **More contrast** (`prefers-contrast: more`): the decorative lines (`border`, `input`) become as strong as secondary text.
- **Less motion** is in the guide **Motion**.

## 7. When to add a colour

The system does not have success, warning or info colours, and a status is a symbol and a word. A second colour is justified only when a page must be scanned in under a second (monitoring, dense tables), and then it is a **new role** (Tokens / Overview §6) with a symbol beside it
(WCAG 1.4.1: colour alone may not carry information, and a green and red pair needs another cue even at 3:1), measured for contrast and for colour-vision deficiency before it is used.

## 8. Routine for a change to colour

1. Edit the token in `theme.css`, light **and** dark.
2. `npm test`: `theme-contrast.test.ts` fails when a text pair is under 4.5:1, a mark under 3:1, a series too close to another (also for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia) or outside the gamut.
3. Update the numbers in **Tokens / Overview**.
4. Look at the Storybook Accessibility panel in light and dark, and at hover, focus, selected, disabled and error states, not only the state at rest.
5. Emulate forced colours, more contrast and 200 % zoom (Chrome DevTools, Rendering; Playwright `emulateMedia({ forcedColors: 'active', contrast: 'more' })`).
6. Look at a chart: are the series named, is the gap kept, is anything told by colour alone?
