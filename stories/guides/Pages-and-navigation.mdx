# Pages and navigation

How a place with a lot in it is built so that none of it is in the way. The rules come from a few things that hold for any
page that is **scanned and operated** rather than read from top to bottom: the summary goes before the detail; state is told in
form (a symbol and words), not only in colour; structure carries information; and what matters is on the page at rest,
never behind a click that only reveals it.

## 1. Three levels, no more

**Front page → list → detail.**

- **Front page**: a **digest**: the state in counts, then the few things that need you most. It answers *«Må jeg gjøre noe nå?»*
- **List**: **everything** of one kind, complete, in groups by what needs you most. It answers *«Hva er det som finnes?»*
- **Detail**: **one thing**, with enough to decide what to do without leaving. It answers *«Hva er dette, og hva gjør jeg?»*

A fourth level means the structure is wrong: make it a section on the detail page, or a list of its own.

## 2. The front page is a digest

1. **Summary first.** A `SummaryBar` of counts, each a link to what it counts, before any list.
2. **A few, by need, not by time.** Each section shows the three to five items that need the reader most (blocked before waiting,
   the oldest review before the newest), then `more` («Se alle 14»). Never the whole list: that is the list page's job.
3. **One screen of what matters, two at most on a phone.** A section with nothing for the reader leaves out its list: one quiet
   line («Ingen å se på») or nothing, never an empty box.
4. **Complete at rest.** What matters is visible without a click. «Se alle» is *navigation* to a page, never a «vis mer» that
   opens a drawer in place.
5. **State in form.** A symbol and words, and `destructive` only for what stops something and is the reader's to fix.
6. **Things that belong together are one group** (`SectionGroup`), with an icon in front of its title.

## 3. Lists

- One page per kind (`Pull requests`, `Issues`), with **all** of it, in the same groups as the digest, most urgent first.
- Reached from the tabs and from every «Se alle».
- A row is: the kind as an icon, a **name that links to the detail page**, one line of where it is from, a `StatusLine`, and
  when it last moved at the end.
- A very long group is cut at ten with a count of the rest and a way to the rest, not scrolled for ever.

## 4. Detail pages

**When:** the thing has more to say than a row can (a description, a history, a set of checks) *and* the reader needs it to
decide. A thing that is a line (an hour of the forecast) has no detail page.

**Anatomy, in this order:**

1. `BackLink`, naming the list («Pull requests»).
2. `PageHeader`: the title, and one line of where it is from and when.
3. `StatusLine`: how it stands.
4. What it is: an excerpt of its text, and the facts as a `FactList`.
5. What happened last: the latest few items of its history.
6. The actions, and **last, the link out** («Åpne på GitHub»).

A detail page has an address of its own (`/pull-requests/owner/repo/12`), so it can be linked to, reloaded and opened from a
notification. It never lives only in a dialog.

## 5. Links

1. **Internal first.** A name in a row links to the app's **own** page for it when there is one. Leaving is a choice the reader
   makes on the detail page, not what a tap on a row does.
2. **A link out is marked and says where.** `ExternalLink` and `LinkButton external` carry the external symbol and are read as
   external, and the text names the place: «Åpne på GitHub», never «Les mer» or «Klikk her».
3. **A link out comes last** on a page, once for each place it goes to.
4. **Where there is no page of our own,** an `ExternalLink` in running text is right: a credit such as «Værdata fra Yr».
5. **Links say what they open.** «Se alle pull requests», «Pull requests», never «Mer».
6. **Same tab.** An installed app has no tabs; a link out opens in the same window and is marked, so the reader knows.

## 6. Navigation

- `PageTabs` between the sibling pages of a place, **in the same order on every page of the set**, the current one marked, with a
  count after a name where there is something to count. At most six, one noun each. No menu behind a button.
- `BackLink` one level up, on every detail page.
- Counts in `SummaryBar` and `PageTabs` are links to what they count.

## 7. Words

Short noun titles; counts as numbers; «Se alle 14»; errors say what went wrong and what to do. Write from the reader's side of
the screen: name things by what they recognise («Venter på din review»), not by how the system is built.

## Don't

- Don't show the whole list on the front page and again on the list page.
- Don't hide what matters behind «vis mer».
- Don't make a tap on a row leave the app when the app has a page for it.
- Don't put a modal where a page with an address would do.
- Don't mark everything urgent: `destructive` is for what stops something.
