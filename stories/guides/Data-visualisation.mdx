# Data visualisation

Our charts are `AreaChart`, `ColumnChart`, `StackedColumnChart`, `DonutChart` and `RankedBarChart`, in a `ChartFrame` or a `ChartCard`. The
palette is grey on purpose: the one colour (`destructive`) is for what stops something, so colour never carries the meaning.

## 1. Choose

| The reader wants | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A trend over time | `AreaChart` |
| Compare a few categories | `ColumnChart` |
| A ranking | `RankedBarChart` |
| A total split into parts over time | `StackedColumnChart` |
| One whole split into a few parts | `DonutChart` |
| One number | A `SummaryBar` or a `FactList`, not a chart |
| Exact values | A table |

## 2. Limits (Primer)

Line 5 series or fewer; bars 10 or fewer; stacked 5 segments; a donut 5 slices with the rest folded into «Annet». More than that is a table.

## 3. Without colour

Name the series on the chart before using a legend; separate neighbouring parts with a thin gap in the background colour; lines are 2 px,
the first solid and later ones dashed with another marker. Marks meet 3:1 against the background (the lightest chart grey needs an outline) and
axis text 4.5:1. A legend is only for more than one series that is not named directly.

## 3b. Title and labels

The title says what the chart shows, with the unit and the period: «Temperatur siste 24 timer: fra −2° til 4°» (Carbon: titles state the insight).
Axes show their units. Figures use tabular numbers.

## 4. Access

The chart draws; the numbers are in a `ChartTable` next to it that a screen reader moves through, and the chart takes no focus. A tooltip repeats
what the table has and is for a pointer. A slice too small to reach is only reachable in the table (Carbon).

## 5. States

Loading is a skeleton at the chart's final height, so the page does not jump. Empty is a sentence that says why, with no axes drawn. An error
says what failed, in the part that failed.
