# Data visualisation

Our charts are `AreaChart`, `LineChart`, `ColumnChart`, `StackedColumnChart`, `DonutChart` and `RankedBarChart`, in a `ChartFrame` or a `ChartCard`, and a `Sparkline` for a shape beside a number. The default
series are the foreground at falling strength (100, 66 and 44 %; a fourth series takes `series-4` and on) and the one colour in the interface (`destructive`) is for what stops something, so colour never carries the meaning. A page that needs colours of its own passes `series-1` to `series-6`
(they are told apart in every kind of colour vision; see **Tokens / Overview**). The rules are from GOV.UK (Analysis Function), Carbon, Primer, NN/G and Datawrapper; what is our own decision is marked.

## 1. Ask the question first

| The reader wants | Use | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One number | A `SummaryBar` or a `FactList`, not a chart | |
| A number and how it moved | The number with a `Sparkline` beside it | |
| A trend over time, one series | `AreaChart` (a line over a light fill) | |
| Trends for up to four series | `LineChart`: the first line solid, the others dashed, each named at its last point | |
| Compare a few categories | `ColumnChart` | |
| A ranking | `RankedBarChart`, sorted, longest first | |
| A total split into parts over time | `StackedColumnChart` | |
| How one whole divides | `DonutChart` only with five parts or fewer and one that dominates; otherwise a ranking | |
| Exact values | A table (`showTable`) | |

**Not drawn** (GOV.UK, NN/G, Datawrapper): 3D charts, radial gauges (a linear `Progress` is the bullet), a pie or donut of more than five slices, two value axes in one chart, a truncated bar axis, colour as the measure of size, and a chart of fewer than three points (the number says it better).
More than four series is a table, or one `AreaChart` each in a list, which fits the one-column rule better than a grid of small charts.

## 2. Limits

Line five series or fewer (GOV.UK says four, Primer five); bars ten or fewer; a stack five segments (GOV.UK four); a donut five slices, and the `keep` prop cannot be more. More than that is a ranking or a table.

## 3. Anatomy

- **Title and subtitle.** The title states the message («Avspillingene økte gjennom høsten»), the subtitle gives what is counted, the unit and the period (GOV.UK, Datawrapper, Carbon). `ChartCard` has both. Say the unit in the subtitle and again in the tooltip.
- **Bars start at zero**, and the axis is never broken (GOV.UK). `ColumnChart` and `RankedBarChart` do: bars of 100 and 102 differ by two percent in height. A line may leave zero if the break is said in the text.
- **Order.** A ranking is sorted by value; time, weekday and hour have their own order. In a stack, the part that matters most is first or last.
- **Labels are horizontal**; if they do not fit, use `RankedBarChart` (horizontal bars) or `labelEvery` (GOV.UK, Datawrapper). Numbers have a decimal comma, grouping with a space and a real minus (`formatChartValue`: `nb-NO`).
- **Name the series on the chart** before a legend; a legend is for several series that are not named directly (GOV.UK, Primer, Carbon).
- **Few annotations**, short and close to the data, and the same words in the text alternative (Datawrapper, GOV.UK).
- **Light gridlines**, and no frame filled with elements (GOV.UK, Carbon).

## 4. Without colour

Name the series; separate neighbouring parts with a thin gap in the background colour; lines are 2 px, the first solid and later ones dashed with another marker (Primer). Marks meet 3:1 against the background and axis text 4.5:1: the default steps keep 3:1 on the page and the card (a test checks it; lighter steps do not, and are not used). Name the series and keep the gap anyway (see **Tokens / Overview**). A chart's colour never carries a state: use `series-*` or the foreground, and tell a state with a symbol and words.

## 5. A number with its shape

<span className="sb-unstyled">14 avspillinger <Sparkline values={[4, 6, 5, 9, 12, 10, 14]} label="Avspillinger siste sju dager: økte fra 4 til 14" /></span>

A row of a dashboard is the number, its `Sparkline` and a few words («opp 3 fra i går»), and the exact values are wherever the number is explained. A sparkline has no axes and no tooltip; its `label` says what it counts, the period and how it moved.

## 6. Text alternative

Every chart has its numbers as a table (`ChartTable`; `showTable` makes it a table anyone can open) and can have a visible `summary` that says the type, the range, the trend and the extremes without repeating the title or listing every point. `summariseSeries()` writes one when nobody has. See **Accessible charts**.

## 7. States

- **Empty:** a sentence that says why, at the chart's height, no axes drawn: «Ingen data i denne perioden.» (`emptyMessage`). A chart of zero and negative values only (a donut) is empty too.
- **Loading:** a `SectionSkeleton` at the chart's final height inside a `StreamedSection`. **Error:** an `ErrorMessage` in the part. **Stale:** a `Freshness` line. See **Charts on the server**.
- **One value or all equal:** say it in words (`summariseSeries`: «Alle er 5»); a single point is still drawn as a dot, but the number is the better chart.
